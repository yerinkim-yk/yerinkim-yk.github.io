I study autonomous decision-making in settings where agents must reason about control performance, uncertainty, and the inferences made by others.

I am interested in control and optimization for multi-agent systems, with current work on deception and information-aware decision-making. My research asks how policies and controllers can balance system objectives with what an observer learns from their behavior.

Research

Current directions

LQ Control · Sequential Hypothesis Testing

Deception in Discrete-Time Linear-Quadratic Control

How can a controller retain strong performance while making its behavior harder for an observer to identify? This work studies deceptive controllers for discrete-time linear-quadratic systems under adversarial detection constraints, uses sequential hypothesis testing, and characterizes the resulting tradeoff.

Paper

Markov Decision Process · Inverse Reinforcement Learning · Moving Target Defense

Metrics and Tactics for Deception in Sequential Decision Systems

This research develops regularized policy-optimization formulations for deception in multi-agent Markov decision processes against inverse-reinforcement-learning adversaries. It examines diversionary, targeted, and equivocal strategies and their effect on both task performance and an observer’s inference.
